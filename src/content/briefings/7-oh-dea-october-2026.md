## The short version

- **7-OH is not federally illegal yet, but it is close.** On October 14, 2026 the DEA formally announced it intends to place 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) in Schedule I, the strictest federal category, when a product contains it above a set level.
- **The earliest it can happen is November 13, 2026.** The ban starts the day the DEA publishes its order, with no grace period. It lasts two years, with one possible one-year extension, while permanent scheduling is worked out.
- **It targets concentrated and "boosted" products,** using a two-part test explained below.
- **This is separate from the MGM-15, MGM-16 and "pseudo" ban.** Those lab-made compounds have been in Schedule I since August 26, 2026. That ban is in force now.
- **Natural kratom leaf and mitragynine have never been federally scheduled.** This action does not change that, but state laws still apply.

## Which products would be covered

The proposal covers a product when its 7-OH (added together with any pseudo, if both are present) meets **either** of these two tests:

1. **Concentration test:** more than **1 milligram per gram** in powders, capsules, tablets and other solids, or more than **1 milligram per milliliter** in liquids and shots. (1 mg per gram is the same as 0.1%.)
2. **Ratio test:** the product's mitragynine divided by its 7-OH is **less than 100**. In plain terms: if a product does not have at least 100 times as much mitragynine as 7-OH, it is covered, no matter how small the total amount of 7-OH is.

Failing either test is enough. Products that pass both stay outside Schedule I, but the DEA notes that neither DEA nor HHS has found these substances safe at below-threshold levels.

### How to check a product with its lab report (COA)

- Find **7-OH** and **mitragynine** on the certificate of analysis, in the same units (mg per gram, or percent).
- **Test 1:** is 7-OH above 1 mg/g (0.1%)? If yes, it is covered.
- **Test 2:** divide mitragynine by 7-OH. Is the answer below 100? If yes, it is covered.
- **Example, with made-up numbers to show the math:** a COA showing mitragynine at 15 mg/g and 7-OH at 0.1 mg/g passes Test 1 (0.1 is under 1) and passes Test 2 (15 ÷ 0.1 = 150, which is over 100). A tablet showing 7-OH at 20 mg/g fails Test 1 immediately.
- **A COA that doesn't list 7-OH can't answer the question.** Ask your supplier for a current one that reports both numbers. That goes for leaf products too: the ratio test is why the lab numbers matter even for products sold as natural leaf.

## What is not changing

- **Natural kratom leaf and mitragynine** are not federally scheduled. DEA's 2016 attempt to schedule them was withdrawn, and no Federal Register rule has scheduled either one since.
- **State laws are separate and can be stricter.** Several states already ban kratom or 7-OH outright. Check your state at [ikratom.org/banned](/banned).
- **The August 26 order stays in force** until DEA replaces it. When the new order publishes, pseudo moves to the same threshold test as 7-OH, and MGM-15 and MGM-16 stay in Schedule I with no threshold.

## One wrinkle for natural leaf: trace "pseudo"

- **The August 26 order lists pseudo (MGPI) with no threshold.** Until DEA replaces it, any detectable amount is Schedule I on paper, and some tests can report trace MGPI in ordinary leaf products because of processing, storage or lab conditions.
- **The Justice Department says it won't prosecute trace amounts.** Its Aug 25, 2026 announcement says it "will exercise enforcement discretion when only incidental trace amounts of MGPI are confirmed in a product otherwise consistent with botanical kratom." It also says this "does not create a legal exemption." ([justice.gov press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds))
- **A federal lawsuit asks a court to make that binding.** The American Kratom Association sued DOJ and DEA (U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, Case No. 1:26-cv-02997, filed September 2026), asking the court to confirm the order doesn't reach traditional leaf products over trace MGPI. AKA is named here because it is the party that filed the suit.
- **The October proposal would fix this going forward.** It moves pseudo to the same two-part threshold test as 7-OH, so trace amounts would fall below it once the new order publishes.
- **For shops:** ask suppliers for COAs that report pseudo (MGPI) as well as 7-OH and mitragynine, and keep them on file.

## If you run a shop: how to prepare

1. **Pull pseudo, MGM-15 and MGM-16 products now.** They have been Schedule I since August 26, 2026.
2. **List every 7-OH product you carry,** including tablets, shots and anything sold as "enhanced" or "extra strength."
3. **Get a current COA for each one** that reports both 7-OH and mitragynine, and run the two tests above.
4. **Stop reordering anything that fails either test,** and plan to have it off your shelves before November 13, 2026. Once the order publishes there is no grace period, and holding covered stock becomes a federal offense.
5. **Keep records:** COAs, invoices, lot numbers, and what you pulled and when.
6. **Ask your suppliers in writing** how each product measures against both tests.
7. **Talk to a lawyer** about how to dispose of stock you can no longer sell.
8. **Check your state's rules as well.** State law can be stricter than federal law.

## If you use kratom or 7-OH products: what to know

- **Natural leaf is not affected by this federal action.** Your state's law still applies.
- **If you use a concentrated 7-OH product, plan now.** Once the order publishes, possessing a covered product is a federal offense.
- **Don't stockpile** covered products.
- **If you use 7-OH daily, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before you change how you use it.** Stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal.
- **Free, confidential help is available 24/7:** SAMHSA's National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357.

## What happens next

- DEA may publish the temporary order on or after **November 13, 2026**. It takes effect the day it is published.
- The order lasts **two years**, with one possible one-year extension, while DEA works on permanent scheduling. Permanent scheduling requires a full rulemaking with a public comment period.
- iKratom is watching the Federal Register and will update this report, and alert members, as soon as an order is published.
