## What we got wrong

Some of our news summaries and policy alerts stated that the DEA had placed **7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH)** into Schedule I. **That is not true, and we're sorry.** Two of those alerts went out as push notifications.

A reader emailed us to point it out. They were right, and we'd rather say so plainly than quietly edit and move on.

## What is actually true

We verified all of this directly against the Federal Register, the government's own record:

- **7-OH is NOT federally scheduled.** It remains a **proposal only** (Federal Register document `2026-13580`). Nothing has been finalized.
- **The public comment period on the proposed 7-OH threshold is still open — it closes September 10, 2026** (document `2026-17409`). If you want a say, there is still time.
- **What the DEA actually did** on August 26, 2026 was place three *different* compounds in Schedule I: **mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16** (document `2026-17429`).
- **Natural leaf kratom has never been federally scheduled**, and neither has plain **mitragynine**. One of our alerts said otherwise. It was wrong.

State-level bans are a separate matter and several are real — those were reported correctly.

## How it happened

We summarize news from real outlets. In this case a news article itself stated the wrong thing, and our summarizer repeated it, our alert engine promoted it, and it went out as a notification. In one older case our own AI went further than its source and described a ban that did not exist anywhere.

"Only use real sources" turned out not to be enough on its own. A real source can still be wrong, and we passed that mistake along.

## What we fixed

- **Every affected page is corrected.** We swept our entire archive — over 10,000 news items and 2,900 alerts — and corrected all 18 that conflicted with the federal record. Nothing in the archive contradicts it now.
- **The four incorrect alerts have been corrected and downgraded**, and their calls to action removed.
- **We now check claims against the Federal Register before publishing.** Our summarizer, our in-app article write-ups, and our alert engine all verify federal scheduling claims against the government record first. A claim the record contradicts can no longer be published as fact — and an alert that makes one is held for human review instead of being pushed to you.
- **A daily automated audit** re-checks everything we've published and flags anything that drifts.

## What this means for you

Nothing you need to do. No action you took was wasted — we checked, and **no advocacy emails were sent to legislators based on the incorrect information.**

If you want to act on the live issue: the **7-OH comment period closes September 10, 2026**.

Thank you to the reader who caught this. If you ever see something on iKratom that looks wrong, please tell us — it makes the whole toolbelt more trustworthy.
